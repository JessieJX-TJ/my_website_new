01

Overview

What it is

The Apprentice is an AI agent for mold cost estimation, designed around a different approach to building domain expertise. Rather than relying on a predefined and comprehensive knowledge base, the agent gradually develops its professional skills through interaction with engineers who teach, correct, and refine its calculations.

How it learns

An engineer can ask the agent how a particular cost should be calculated. The Apprentice produces an initial result for the engineer to review, after which the engineer can identify what is correct or incorrect and provide additional guidance. The agent retains this feedback as part of its memory, allowing it to incorporate previous corrections into future calculations. In this sense, the agent is designed to learn its expertise progressively through practice.

What I did

As the UX/UI designer, I focused on giving this learning process a recognizable visual subject. I designed the character, illustrations, and supporting visual language around the idea of an AI apprentice: a young and imperfect learner that is capable of making mistakes, receiving instruction, and gradually improving. The design aims to make the agent's learning state and role understandable while encouraging engineers to engage with it as something they can teach rather than simply a tool that is expected to always be correct.

02

Problem Space

Designing for an AI that is still learning

Unlike conventional domain-specific tools with predefined rules and knowledge, the Apprentice develops its cost-estimation skills through interaction with engineers. Its knowledge is therefore incomplete and continuously evolving, making the agent's role fundamentally different from that of an expert system that is expected to provide correct answers from the start.

Making mistakes part of the interaction

Because the agent learns through feedback, engineers need to review its calculations, identify mistakes, and provide corrections. The interaction therefore needs to accommodate imperfection without making every incorrect result feel like a system failure.

Encouraging users to teach the agent

For the learning process to work, engineers need to be willing to invest effort in correcting and teaching the agent. The challenge is not only to make the AI understandable, but also to establish an appropriate relationship between the engineer and the agent—one in which the AI is perceived as a learner that can improve through guidance.

Use illustration to express an evolving personality

I created a series of illustrations to represent the character in different situations and states. Rather than using illustration only as decoration, I used changes in expression, pose, and context to reinforce the idea of an AI that can make mistakes, receive guidance, and gradually improve.

Make the character the subject of the interaction

I designed the character as a visible subject throughout the product, giving the AI a consistent presence beyond a generic avatar or chatbot icon. This makes the agent's actions and learning process easier to associate with a recognizable entity.

Create a visual basis for teaching and trust

The visual identity was designed to make the agent feel approachable and imperfect rather than overly authoritative. By presenting the Apprentice as a learner, the design aims to create a more appropriate expectation of the interaction: engineers are not simply asking an expert for an answer, but helping an AI gradually acquire domain expertise.

04

Reflection

The project made me consider how an AI's visual identity can shape people's expectations of its capabilities and their willingness to engage with it. By presenting the Apprentice as a learner rather than an expert, the design creates space for human teaching and correction. A remaining question is how this relationship should evolve as the agent becomes more capable: when should it continue to appear as a learner, and when should it begin to act with greater confidence and autonomy?
